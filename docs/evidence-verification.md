# Evidence verification methodology

## Review a real case

1. Identify the responsible authority and procedural stage from the actual record. Distinguish recommendations, committee decisions, council decisions, additional approvals, and effective dates. No universal municipal workflow is assumed.
2. Record the official source title, type, publication date, HTTPS URL, exact passage, and page or section. Preserve document-version wording.
3. Write the smallest supported neutral claim. Keep conditions, documented amendments, and unresolved uncertainty visible. Attach the supporting passages.
4. Verify project summaries and rationales as well as individual claims. Resolve contradictory records before marking a claim Verified; use Needs Verification or Rejected while unresolved. A reviewer must assess the contradiction—the software does not automatically detect contradictory prose.
5. Link public feedback to a response or amendment only if the source establishes that relationship. Missing evidence is not rejection. Aggregate themes avoid unnecessary personal details.
6. Verify participation from its own notice, including dates, offset, IANA time zone, official instructions, and review timestamp. Never infer a deadline from a meeting. Blank/unconfirmed dates remain unverified.
7. Check map provenance and geometry validity. Distinguish source facts, calculated spatial relationships, unverified outcomes, and unavailable analysis.
8. Save edited content, then record evidence and claim verification in a separate save. Review the public preview before approving publication.

## What the software enforces

Zod rejects invalid types, unsafe non-HTTPS URLs, invalid dates/time zones, reversed date ranges, open polygon rings, missing references, unsupported response relationships, and official-confirmation labels on fictional sources. The server invalidates source/claim/participation approvals when their content changes. Optimistic revisions reject stale updates.

Publication requires reviewed supporting evidence for the summary, all claims, timelines, versions, mapped scopes, and sourced feedback. Confirmed opportunities require reviewed evidence and confirmation, plus an official URL for real cases. Unknown opportunities may remain unknown. A source title or numerical confidence score is never substituted for an exact passage.

These are structural safeguards and recorded human attestations. They cannot independently determine whether a domain is municipal, a passage is accurate, an interpretation is complete, or a regulation has legally taken effect.

## Demonstration review

The three synthetic fixtures are labeled fictional and have no official citation URLs. Their review timestamps mean internal consistency review only; synthetic evidence cannot become Officially Confirmed. The UI labels demonstration data at project and source levels.

Real imported cases have official citations, exact excerpts, nullable source dates and explicit verification gaps. Source-supported facts are distinct from unresolved approvals and inaccessible reports. Import review is not an endorsement by the municipality. Computed differences and spatial relationships remain derived results, whether their inputs are official or fictional; they do not establish causation or an official impact boundary.

## Evaluation boundaries

Automated tests exercise evidence linking, status semantics, dates, publication gates, persistence, navigation, and core accessibility behavior. Passing them is not evidence of resident comprehension, legal accuracy, fairness, or formal accessibility compliance. A pilot should record reviewer agreement, citation correctness, preservation of conditions, task success, and observed comprehension using two or three real cases.
